# The Apex

The Apex (working folder and package name: hover-racer) is a futuristic 2D top-down hovercraft drift racer for the browser (TypeScript + Vite + Canvas 2D). It is a standalone project and unrelated to the Elysium MOBA in `C:\Users\AbeBo\code\celtic-moba`.

Project brief (vision, visual reference, art prompts, roadmap): https://claude.ai/code/artifact/aa070345-1401-4a8b-8651-8a145accf77f

## Decisions so far
- Controls: keyboard steering only (no mouse steering): A / D or ← / → steer (`Input.steer`, eased toward full lock by `steerAim` so a tap is a small correction; a bar at the bottom shows it). The throttle is always on. S / ↓ / Space (or left click) brakes, and the gearbox shifts down as speed drops. W / ↑ / Shift / E (or right click) deploys ERS boost; the HUD labels the battery "BOOST (W)". The context menu is suppressed over the canvas. Gears are automatic: 5 gears, shifting up near each gear's top speed and down when the craft slows.
- Walls sit at the track edge; you scrape along them, losing the speed going into the wall plus some friction, with sparks. There is no runoff.
- Slipstream: tucking into a car's wake gives up to +5% pace. It's drawn as flowing dashed draft lines from the follower's nose to the leader's tail.
- ERS: drifting charges a battery. A slide past about 23° of slip at speed charges it, faster the deeper and faster the drift: a hard 1 s drift from 180 km/h gives about 0.25. Harvest is capped at one full battery per lap (`HARVEST_PER_LAP`, reset when a lap completes). Braking does not charge it. Right click (or Shift / E) deploys it: 1.69x pull and +11.25% top speed (25% stronger than the original 1.55x / +9%), draining 0.25/s. Each race starts at 50%. The AI deploys on straights when attacking or defending, or when the battery is over 60%.
- DRS: zones are found automatically on straights at least about 650 px long (`Track.drsZones`). From lap 2, entering a zone within 1.0 s of the car directly ahead opens the wing (+7% top speed) until the zone ends or the car brakes. Gaps come from per-sample pass times (`RaceState.gapBehind`), the same way F1 timing works, and the HUD shows the gap to the car ahead and behind.
- Catch-up: +1% pace (top speed and pull) per place from 3rd back, capped at +3% (`CATCH_UP_PER_PLACE`, `CATCH_UP_MAX`), for every car, the player included. P2 gets nothing, so a clean leader isn't handed to the car right behind (it used to be +1% per place behind the leader, uncapped, which with slipstream and DRS made leading almost a guarantee of being passed). The HUD shows the player's bonus.
- Turbo start (clutch-style): hold the brake (S / Space) through the countdown and release it between 0.3 s before and 0.15 s after GO for a 1.4 s launch at 1.9x pull. Releasing earlier is a jump start and stalls for 0.8 s; never holding the brake is a normal start. The AI roll their own starts (`Driver.rollLaunch`).
- Heat: only walls add heat; car-to-car contact doesn't. At 200 km/h a 10° hit adds about 0.44, a 30° hit about 0.9 (made 60% harsher: `HEAT_PER_IMPACT` 1/350), and anything harder maxes the bar. Grinding along a wall adds 0.85/s. Above 0.5 heat the car loses up to 10% top speed; at 1.0 it overheats (-25%) until it cools below 0.35. It cools at 0.05/s (0.35/s on the cooling lane). Wall hits also cost speed straight away: up to 30% extra on a hard hit (`WALL_HIT_LOSS`), plus grinding friction. The shift points scale with the speed multiplier, so a hot car can't get stuck in a gear.
- AI racecraft (`src/ai.ts`): commits to a passing side (the inside of a coming corner if there's room) for 1.6 s, covers the inside when someone is within about 150 px behind into a corner, and makes occasional mistakes (late braking or running wide; the rate scales with 1 - skill).
- Drift: heading and velocity are separate, so the craft slides. There is no drift boost (removed on purpose).
- Acceleration builds gradually: about 3.7 s to reach 5th gear. Each upshift is a light 0.12 s dip to 65% thrust (a felt shift, not a stall).
- Handling: one model for every craft, player and AI alike (what used to be the ASSISTED option is now simply how hovercraft handle; there is no PRO mode). Grip stays strong at speed (2.6 at a standstill to `GRIP_FAST` 1.45 at top speed; braking loosens it to `BRAKE_GRIP_FAST` 1.2) and only half the sideways momentum is redirected forward (`GRIP_KEEP` 0.5), so turning hard scrubs speed off by itself: the craft slows into corners and holds the road. `Mods.assist` / `Setup.assist` are kept only so old saves and online messages still parse. The AI brakes on its own and steers against its slide (`slipCorrect` 0.5, `CORNER_K` 56, tuned for this model: under it the AI's pace is limited by the physics, about 7% slower than it was when it drove the old momentum-keeping model, and skill matters less), skill 0.82 to 0.97.
- Drawing performance (`src/render.ts`, `fitCanvas` in `main.ts`): the ground and the static track layers are pre-rendered into 512 px world tiles (`drawWorldTiles`, an LRU cache of 60, one extra tile prefetched per frame), and only the animated parts are drawn live (`drawTrackLive`: rail pulses, lights, cooling lane, start gantry). Harbor Lights' moving water is drawn live under transparent tiles (`groundMoves`). The canvas renders at most about 2.2 million pixels (`MAX_PIXELS`), and while racing the resolution drops in steps (down to 0.5x) if frames keep running under 45 fps. Measured with software drawing at 1080p, a frame went from 53 ms to about 28 ms. Anything new that never moves should go into the tiles, not be drawn every frame.
- View: a 3D chase camera (`src/view3d.ts`, three.js) is the default; V switches to the original top-down view (saved in localStorage `the-apex-view`; top-down only if WebGL is missing). The 3D view only draws: the simulation, AI and online code are the same for both. World (x, y) maps to three.js (x, z) with y up. The track is built once per circuit from its samples (deck ribbon, kerbs, barriers with rail pulses and lights, ground glow, cooling lane, DRS lines, start gantry); scenery props become simple 3D shapes in `propObject`; cars are voxel models (4 px cubes, `hullVoxels` / `wingVoxels`, drawn as instanced meshes with a little per-cube shade variation) in their livery colours (`CarRig`: skirt, hull, pods with lit intakes, canopy, wing that tilts for DRS, glowing nozzles with blocky flames, ERS/DRS aura, pixel number decal, underglow and shadow). Slipstream shows as glowing dashes streaming from the follower's nose to the leader's tail in three pinching lanes (`Slipstreams`, one instanced mesh), as in the top-down view, leaning into turns and dipping under braking. The 2D canvas sits on top and draws only the HUD (`Scene.chase`). Steering in both views is the keys (`steerAim`, `Craft.maxTurn`). ASSISTED handling's turn braking was made stronger (`ASSIST_KEEP` 0.5: only half the sideways momentum is redirected, so turning hard scrubs more speed). About 280 draw calls and 3 ms a frame on Neon Streets. In dev, `game.chase.debugCam = { yaw, dist, h }` orbits the player's car for inspecting models.
- The top-down version from before the 3D view is kept as branch `top-down-classic` and tag `top-down-v1`.
- Visual direction (Abe's words): "a future which combines the most beautiful parts of cyberpunk and archeofuturism, combined with Atlantean and aristocratic aesthetics". In practice: deep sea teal, ivory and gold, classical arches, sunbursts and deco corners, with neon in each character's or car's colour as the accent. Names and titles use the Cinzel web font (Georgia fallback).
- Story portraits are drawn in code (`src/portraits.ts`) and folded into origami: each vector bust (thin ink outlines removed) is cut into a Delaunay mesh of flat paper planes, denser where the drawing changes (eyes, mouth, edges); each plane takes the drawing's colour and a tone from its fold angle (brightness as height, lit from the top left), with faint pale creases and a matte grain. The backdrop is a folded paper sunburst under a faceted gold arch with a neon inner line in the character's colour. 400 px, full resolution (no pixel grid). The old PNGs in `public/portraits/` are no longer used but kept. The dialogue box (`storyui.ts`) uses the same look.
- Car selection cards (`.hr-car` in `menu.ts`, `cardFace()`, smaller in the online lobby) are deco arches showing each car's 3D model, rendered once at startup by `renderCarCards` in view3d.ts. Cars have ivory stripes and gold nozzle rims by default.
- Players only get the 3D chase view now: the top-down renderer stays in the code as a fallback when WebGL is missing, and V switches to it in dev builds only.
- Origami look (replaced the pixel/voxel style): the craft is folded paper (`CRAFT_MODEL = 'origami'`: `origamiHull` / `origamiPlate` build a `Fold` of triangles, each tagged with a part kind; `foldMeshes` gives every facet its own tone, draws crease lines with EdgesGeometry, keeps glowing parts unlit, and prints a livery's paper pattern on the painted panels via `liveryPaper`, which uses liveries.ts's `liveryPattern` if present or samples its `liveryPainter` from above). Map props are folded too (`MAP_STYLE = 'origami'`: `PropFolds` rebuilds each shape with few facets via `foldedShape`, tones each facet, adds creases, turns building windows into glowing lantern strips, and merges the whole map into three meshes). The voxel models and `PropVoxels` are kept in the code ('fighter', 'car', `MAP_STYLE = 'voxel'`).
- The craft is a starfighter flying with its wings closed (`CRAFT_MODEL = 'fighter'` in view3d.ts; the earlier F1-style hovercar is kept as 'car'): a long tapered nose with ivory bands, a framed glass canopy, a gold fairing, engine pods with gold intake rings and glowing nozzles (`fighterVoxel`), and on each side two stacked blade wings with gold leading edges, a livery stripe and neon tips (`plateVoxel`) that part slightly when DRS opens. Span about 60 px, length 90; physics and hitboxes are unchanged.
- Voxel look: cars are 2 px cubes (`carVoxel` / `wingVoxel` describe the shape in world px; `sampleVoxels` keeps only surface cubes; about 1,500 cubes a car). Scenery props are built from simple shapes in `propObject` and then converted to world-aligned cubes by `PropVoxels` (cube size = prop size / 16, clamped 5-22 px; windows become lit cube rows; moving parts, signs and see-through effects stay as meshes). The whole map's cubes are two instanced meshes: Neon Streets about 61k cubes, 87 draw calls; Summit Pass about 47k, 4 ms a frame. `Car.skin` (`CarSkin`: body, trim, accent, glow, pattern) recolours a car's model.
- Mythic liveries (`src/liveries.ts`): each unlockable skin has its own pattern, painted cube by cube on the craft's paintwork through an optional painter in `voxelMeshes` (the hull gets top / side faces from `fuselageHalf`; on the wing plates the ivory panel carries the pattern and the stripe the accent). TRITON bronze with sea-green patina, HADES black glass with gold kintsugi cracks, MIDAS gold dragon scales, ZEUS ivory with a glowing lightning bolt down each flank, ATHENA veined marble, HELIOS dawn-red to gold with a sunburst, IRIS opal, HERMES brushed silver with deco chevrons, ATLANTIS teal with a gold Greek-key meander, AUGUSTUS Tyrian purple with gold chevrons and fleurons, DAEDALUS glowing circuit traces, NYX matte black with one red line. `liveryMetal` swaps the gold trim where it clashes. Unlocked skins are picked on the car selection screens: a YOUR SKINS row of cards under the cars, in the same card style, on the Quick Race / Season setup screen and in the online lobby (`skinCards` in menu.ts, `refreshSkinCards` / `equipSkin` in main.ts). Picking a skin wears it on your car; picking a stock car goes back to that car's own colours. The Garage is only the collection now (every skin and the achievement that earns it, plus achievements and the online record). Skin pictures are rendered once each, when first needed (`skinPictures`).
- The 2.5D look is faked: one rotated sprite plus an offset shadow and hover bob for now, with sprite stacking later.
- Visual reference: the mobile drift racer Hot Slide (stylized, bright, readable from above), reimagined with neon and hovercraft.

- Music (`src/music.ts`) is an epic, Celtic-flavoured orchestra synthesized live (original themes, scheduled on a step grid): strings, spiccato/fiddle figures, French horns, tin whistle with grace notes, choir, a pipes drone, taiko, bodhrán, snare rolls and cymbal swells in a 3.6 s hall reverb. Menu: an 80 BPM theme in D Mixolydian (whistle over strings, then horns and choir). Race: a galloping 12/8 jig in E minor with heroic horn calls. Sprint: 132 BPM in A minor with driving spiccato strings and a brass anthem. Each song arranges an 8-bar cycle over 32 bars (opening, build, full with choir, breath ending in a roll). The pause menu muffles the music. Effects and music use separate buses in `src/audio.ts`, so menus silence the engine without stopping the music. Music starts on the first click or key press, since browsers block autoplay.

- Tracks (`TRACKS` in `src/track.ts`), each a place in the game's world: Apex Arena (futurist marble arena), Velocity Colosseum (oval inside a marble colosseum), New York (deco skyline, a colossus with a torch, the elevated line), Lappland (arctic polar night under a moving aurora), New Atlantis (marble islands, sea-glass towers and the crowned lighthouse spire on the sea), Crystal Front (crystal biome of a sci-fi war zone: giant shards, wrecked mechs, a crashed dropship, shield domes, lightning storm), Hieropolis (sacred city of the sun: ziggurat arcology, obelisks, guardian statues, palms, pools) and Hanging Gardens (Babylon-style sky terraces with waterfalls, cypress avenues, gold towers). These names are used everywhere, story missions and lap records included (renamed from Apex Grand Prix, Velocity Ring, Neon Streets, Summit Pass, Harbor Lights, Crystal Canyon, Desert Mirage and Skyline Carousel; old records were dropped). Each starts on a straight heading east and runs counter-clockwise (infield on the left), so the grid, cooling lane and DRS work without per-track setup.

- Sprints (built by both chats): five point-to-point routes raced once, start and finish far apart: Fae Forest, Nova Camelot, Zoropersia, Bifrost, El Dorado (`TrackDef.sprint`, after the eight circuits in `TRACKS`; 71k-88k px, about 85-112 s). Track samples run 0..`routeEnd` along the route (0 = start line), then a 70-sample lead-in where the grid sits; there is no road between routeEnd and the lead-in (`isBreak`), `shift(i, d)` steps along without crossing it, `finishIndex` is 1600 px before the end (a run-off and an end barrier follow), `fraction(i)` / `routeLength` measure progress, and RaceState finishes the run at the finish line. Routes are generated (rows across the world joined by U-turns) and checked with per-sample road widths. At every jump the road splits (`placeJumps`): it eases apart over 900 px into two 280 px lanes around an island (`Track.outer` / `Track.island` per sample), one lane has the ramp (70 px high) and a 600 px gap (`Jump.side`), the other is a flat bypass, and they rejoin after the landing run. collideWalls handles both walls, the island and the end barrier, and sets `craft.ground`. Jumps glide (GRAVITY 380, about 1.5 s in the air), airtime charges the boost (`ERS_AIR`), and the harvest allowance refreshes every 3 sectors. The AI picks a lane per split (the jump about 80% of the time, never cutting across an island's nose) and brakes to a stop after the finish. In 3D the road is drawn in two halves (centre to edge) so ramps and gaps sit in their lane, islands are raised blocks with landmarks on them, START and FINISH gantries, and on these big maps the sky travels with the camera. The other chat's side: one run regardless of laps, the SPRINTS row and distance, km/progress strip/DRS by fraction, the open minimap with the finish marked, landing effects, the autopilot after the finish, altitude online and in ghosts; sprints stay out of seasons.
- Sprint forks: every fork offers two different lanes (`LaneKind`): a jump (a glide that charges the boost), boost pads (+10% top and 1.45x pull while on them and 0.9 s after, `Craft.pad`), a charging strip (+0.45 battery/s, outside the harvest cap), a cooling strip (sheds heat like the cooling lane, via `inCoolingLane`), a fire lane (boost pads that add heat), or a plain road. Forks sit on straights (`placeJumps`, taking each track's `TrackDef.forks` recipe in turn and alternating sides) and on the sweeping U-turns (`placeCurveForks`: the island follows the bend, so the inside lane is shorter but tighter and the outside wider, using `curveForks` [inside, outside]). Effects come from `Track.strips` and are applied in collideWalls; `forkAt` / `stripAt` look them up. Each sprint has its own mix (Zoropersia fire vs cooling, El Dorado cooling vs charging, Bifrost jumps and boosts...), 12-15 forks each. The AI rates each lane by what it offers and what it needs (battery, heat; the inside line for strong drivers) with a little randomness, and takes the inside of a turn fork a bit slower. In 3D each strip glows in its own way (gold chevrons, flames, charging pulses, frost) and a sign on each island's nose names both lanes.
- Map landmarks: `build()` in scenery.ts places them per track (`row` beside the track, `clumps`, `scatter`); `propObject` in view3d.ts models each kind from simple shapes (boxes, cylinders, cones, spheres, octahedra), which `PropVoxels` turns into cubes, so keep parts at least about 1/10 of the prop's size or they vanish. Palette constants: MARBLE, GOLD_C, SEA_GLASS, SANDSTONE, PATINA. Per-track sky, light, haze and ground tint live in `ATMOS` (view3d.ts); `aurora` and `lightning` add sky effects that follow the camera (`skyFollow`). Measured: 21k-64k cubes and 60-118 draw calls per map, 0.5-5.7 ms a frame.
- Scenery (`src/scenery.ts`): each track has its own ground texture (a generated tile, drawn as a repeating pattern) and props, built once per track with a seeded generator and always kept clear of the walls, so they never affect driving. Tall props fake 2.5D height by leaning away from the camera centre (`LEAN`). Overlays (the Harbor Lights lighthouse beam, the Velocity Ring blimp) are drawn above the cars. To give a new track scenery, add a `case` for its name in `build()`.

- Front menu: four windows (Story, Quick Race, Online, Season), each with its own animated CSS scene, growing on hover. Quick Race and Season open the setup screen locked to that mode.
- Online (`src/net.ts`, `src/lobbyui.ts`, and the "online play" section of `main.ts`): peer to peer with PeerJS (its free public server only introduces the browsers). The host gets a 5-letter code, guests join with it, and everyone picks a car in the lobby; the host sets mode (single race or a season of 2 to 8), laps, track and AI cars (default: fill the grid to 6). The host's browser runs the race: guests send their controls (aim, brake, boost) every frame, and the host sends a snapshot of every car 30 times a second, which guests draw 0.1 s behind with interpolation. A guest's own car is predicted locally so it responds instantly: every control has a seq, the host applies exactly one per physics step, and each snapshot carries that guest's full car state (`Craft.getState`) plus the last seq applied; the guest resets to it, replays newer controls, and blends any difference in (snapping only above 150 px). Prediction includes walls, slipstream and bumps against the other cars projected to the present (`bumpOff`, `GHOST_LATENCY`). Measured in a 6-car pack, corrections were typically 0 px and at most about 13 px. The host also sends its results screen (with season standings) for guests to show. The host can turn CONTACT off in the lobby (`LobbyState.contact`, `noContact()` in main.ts): cars then pass through each other, on the host and in each guest's prediction. If a guest leaves mid-race, an AI takes over their car; if the host leaves, guests go back to the menu. Pausing online doesn't stop the race. The host's tab has to stay visible (browsers slow hidden tabs).
- Hosting: GitHub Pages at https://abrahambouchard24.github.io/the-apex/ (repo abrahambouchard24/the-apex). `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` builds and publishes on every push to master. Assets use relative paths (Vite `base: './'`).
- Story mode, "Rookie Season" (`src/story.ts` for data and logic, `src/storyui.ts` for screens): 11 missions in 4 chapters, built from the normal race with per-mission field, grid (back, pole or random), laps, start heat, AI pace buff and goals (win, top N, beat a named character, lap time, never overheat). Characters: the player (typed name), Charlie (county legend and first sponsor), Mr. Lupis (Team Secretary, Leopard GP), Sophi (team mechanic), Judah (arch-rival, Vanta Racing, a fixed pace edge via `Entrant.edge`) and Rook (Judah's teammate). Every Story rival gets 80% of the player's garage upgrades (`UPGRADE_MATCH`); Judah on his debut (`matchPlayer`) gets 100%, so only near-perfect driving beats him there. Post-race lines can be conditional (`Line.if`: goals, `not`, `winner`, or a remembered story `flag` such as 'beat-judah'), so the dialogue matches what happened; `{place}` fills in the finishing place. Points from missions buy garage upgrades (`UPGRADES`: 1-3% per level, up to 10 levels). Replays pay 25%. Progress is saved in localStorage (`the-apex-story`). The story screen only ever shows the next mission (a CONTINUE card) and the missions already finished; anything further ahead stays hidden. Each chapter opens with a full-screen title card the first time. Retry after a loss skips the briefing and intro. Portraits load from `public/portraits/<who>.png`, with initials shown until the art exists. Difficulty was checked by simulating each mission with an AI stand-in for the player; the late missions need about level-4 upgrades plus strong driving.
- Car tuning (`src/setup.ts`, `src/tuneui.ts`): four trade-off sliders, -3 to +3 steps, the centre being the standard car. Gearing (+1.5% top speed / -3% pull per step toward long), Wing (+5% grip / -1% top speed per step toward high), Cooling (+8% heat resistance / -0.75% top speed per step toward big), ERS mode (+8% boost power / -12% boost charge per step toward power). Presets: Balanced, Speed, Grip, Endurance. Saved in localStorage (`the-apex-setup`), opened with TUNE CAR from the Quick Race / Season setup screen, the Online lobby and the Story screen. It applies to the player in every mode (stacking on Story upgrades); online each guest's setup travels in `hello` / `setup` messages and the host applies it to their car. In simulation, speed setups win on Velocity Colosseum, Lappland and New Atlantis, and grip setups on the twistier tracks. The AI carries more corner speed with more grip (`gripGain` in ai.ts).
- Settings (`src/settings.ts`, screen in `src/settingsui.ts`; from the front menu and the pause menu): music and effects volume (separate gain nodes in `audio.ts`), graphics quality (High / Medium / Low: render-resolution cap, shadow map size, and on Low no shadows or speed lines), fullscreen, and rebindable keys for steer left / right, brake and boost (mouse buttons always brake and boost). Saved in localStorage (`the-apex-settings`), along with mute and whether the tutorial is done.
- Records: the best lap per track from Quick Race and Season is saved (`the-apex-records`) and shown on the track cards. Story, online and tutorial laps don't count.
- Time Trial (TIME TRIAL on the front menu; `src/ghost.ts`): the setup screen in trial mode, alone on track against a ghost of your best lap on that track. Laps from Quick Race, Season and Time Trial are recorded 30 times a second (`GhostRecorder`), and a lap that beats the saved ghost replaces it (`the-apex-ghosts`; a standing-start first lap only counts when there is no ghost yet). The ghost is drawn in the 3D view as a faint sea-teal hologram (`Car.ghost`, `ghostly()` in view3d.ts), never collides or counts in positions, and lap toasts show the time against it.
- Installable app: `public/manifest.webmanifest` (full screen, landscape, deco icons in `public/icons/` drawn by a script: gold apex chevron on deep teal) and `public/sw.js`, registered in production builds only. The service worker serves pages network-first (a new deploy shows up straight away) and build files and fonts cache-first, so the game plays offline; PeerJS traffic is never cached. When the browser offers an install, an INSTALL button appears on the front menu (`beforeinstallprompt`). `npm run build` then the `hover-racer-dist` launch config (vite preview on 4174) tests it.
- Touch controls (`src/touch.ts`): on a touch screen, once it has been touched, racing shows steer left / right buttons under the left thumb, BRAKE and BOOST under the right (left of the dash) and a pause button, in the deco style. They press the same controls as the keys (`Input.setTouch`), so steering eases in the same way. On phone-sized screens (under 560 px tall) the dash, minimap, lap and gap panels shrink (`hudScale`), the keyboard help line hides while touch controls show, and the front menu keeps its four windows side by side with just their titles.
- Daily challenge (`src/daily.ts`, DAILY CHALLENGE on the front menu): one 3-lap race a day, the same for everyone, picked from the local date with a seeded generator: track, car, AI skills and a twist (start from the back and finish top 3; start at 70% heat and finish top 2; or a 3% faster field from pole, win). - Track colour themes match each world (Nov 2026): walls, kerbs, lamps and road tint follow the place, in the teal / ivory / gold palette with one accent: Apex Arena teal and gold, Velocity Colosseum gold on marble, New York brass with teal kerbs, Lappland aurora mint on snow, New Atlantis sea-glass and gold, Crystal Front violet crystal, Hieropolis sun-gold with terracotta, Hanging Gardens leaf green on limestone with gold kerbs, Fae Forest fae green and violet, Nova Camelot gold with royal blue, Zoropersia flame with turquoise tile, Bifrost rainbow violet, El Dorado gold with jungle green.
- Restart is hold R for 0.8 s (`RESTART_HOLD`), with a bar on screen, so a stray tap can't wipe a race.
- Tutorial (`src/tutorial.ts`, HOW TO PLAY on the front menu, highlighted until finished once): a solo 2-lap race on Apex Arena with one prompt at a time (turbo start, steering, braking, drifting to charge boost, boosting, heat and the cooling lane, DRS), each moving on once the player has done it.
- 3D feel (`view3d.ts`): cars cast real shadows from each track's sun (a small shadow box that follows the player), the road has a glossy specular sheen, wind streaks rush past from about 150 km/h (stronger on boost), the camera rumbles slightly from about 180 km/h, and boosting pulls the camera back and widens the view. Cars passing the player (or being passed) at speed play a panned whoosh with a Doppler pitch drop (`Sound.passBy`).
- Car stats: `Craft.mods` (top speed, pull, grip, heat resistance, boost) is how upgrades and Judah's edge work.

## Layout
- `src/craft.ts` holds the physics and pure game logic, with no DOM access.
- `src/input.ts` handles the mouse (with pointer lock) and the keyboard.
- `src/track.ts` holds the circuits in `TRACKS` (each a Catmull-Rom spline through control points, with a color theme), walls and lap/sector timing. Eight tracks (see Tracks above). Lappland's ridge esses and descent S-bend were eased because the AI kept hitting the walls there (about 38 wall hits a race in a 6-car AI race, about 14 after). Keep corner radii above about 255 px and separate parts of a track at least about 540 px apart, or the walls misbehave.
- `src/ai.ts` holds the AI driver: look-ahead steering with an inside-line bias and corner-speed braking. `CORNER_K` and `skill` set its pace (skill 0.75 is about 30.5 s a lap).
- `src/render.ts` does all canvas drawing (track, kerbs, minimap, HUD, arrow cursor); it only reads game state.
- `src/menu.ts` holds the DOM overlays: start screen (car, track, mode, laps and season length), pause menu and the results screen (with season standings).
- Race setup: single race or a season of 2, 3, 4, 6 or 8 races, and 1, 3, 5 or 10 laps. A season starts on the chosen track and rotates through the others; points go 10/8/6/4/2/1 for 1st-6th, ties broken by the latest race. Season logic lives in `main.ts` (`Season`, `scoreRound`, `resultsView`).
- `src/audio.ts` synthesizes all sound with Web Audio (engine, upshift whoosh, wall scrape and thuds, countdown beeps, lap chimes); M mutes. No sound files.
- `src/main.ts` runs the fixed 60 Hz loop, the camera, particles and the race flow (menu → countdown → race → results). In dev, `window.game` has `advance(seconds)` to step the game while the tab is hidden and `autodrive()` to let the AI drive your car.

Run with `npm run dev`. Done so far: one circuit with kerbs, runoff, walls, 10 sector checkpoints and lap timing; and a 3-lap, 6-car race: the player's red 01 against 5 AI cars (cyan 02, green 3, orange 4, purple 5, blue 6), each with a random skill from 0.7 to 0.9, starting from a shuffled grid, with a countdown, positions, car-to-car contact and a finish banner. Car art lives in `public/sprites/` and is trimmed to its visible pixels at load time.
